# Sources

## Manuscripts

All are in the Archivo General de Simancas, Patronato Real, leg. 2, and all can be seen on PARES, the Spanish
archives' portal. The number in the second column is the PARES description, which goes at the end of
`https://pares.cultura.gob.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/description/`. Image numbers are those of the PARES
viewer.

| shelfmark | PARES | what | used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| PTR,LEG,2,1,19 | 12987122 | The cardinal to Charles, Valladolid, 9 September 1520. Six images. The catalogue has "parcialmente cifrada". | the paragraph, on image 1 |
| PTR,LEG,2,1,99 | 12987701 | The cardinal to Charles, 5 December 1521. Cipher on images 1-5, the decipherment of 1521 on images 9-10. | letter A |
| PTR,LEG,2,1,100 | 12987720 | The cardinal to Charles, Vitoria, 7 December 1521, on the Duke of Nájera's request that the king pay his troops for the defence of Torrelobatón. Five lines in cipher on image 1, with a decipherment in the margin. With enclosures, among them an undated sheet wholly in cipher, images 3-5, and a letter partly in cipher on the quarrel with the Admiral of Castile over the new bishop of Osma. | letter B (the sheet) |
| PTR,LEG,2,1,101 | 12987723 | The cardinal to Charles, 7 December 1521. Cipher on images 1-2, the decipherment of 1521 on image 3. | letter C and the first test |
| PTR,LEG,2,1,76 | 12987566 | Catalogued as the cardinal to Charles, 6 May 1521. A sheet marked *Duplicadas* on the back, copying the letters of 1 and 6 May 1521. Image 5 has the end of the first and the copy of the second, with a cipher paragraph written into it. | letter D |
| PTR,LEG,2,1,23 | 12987157 | The cardinal to Charles, Valladolid, 8 October 1520. Cipher words in a paragraph added after the first part was written, image 3. | the second test |

The older shelfmark of the letter of 9 September 1520 is Comunidades de Castilla, leg. 2, fols 157-158, and
that is how Danvila cites it. The catalogue counts three leaves, a postscript recommending various persons
being on a leaf of its own, and calls the hand *gótica cortesana*.

Nos 100 and 101 are two letters of 7 December 1521 on different matters, and of no. 100 only the enclosed
sheet was used.

Other records bear on these letters, though none supplied anything to the key.

- PTR,LEG,2,1,94 (PARES 12987679) is the letter of 24 October 1521, and the catalogue gives PTR,LEG,2,1,92 as
  its decipherment. Danvila's print of its cipher paragraphs was enough to align letter B.
- PTR,LEG,2,1,22 (PARES 12987154), the letter of 1 October 1520, speaks of "la cifra que va dentro desta" as
  something enclosed.

No decipherment is filed with the letter of 9 September 1520. The leaf before it in the old foliation,
fol. 156, holds deciphered paragraphs of a letter of the cardinal's, which Danvila prints under the heading
"Lo que se sacó de la cifra de la carta del Cardenal" (vol. 1, p. 373) and Höfler on p. 9. Their matter is
the risings of June 1520, and they have nothing of this paragraph.

## Printed editions of the letter

- Constantin von Höfler, "Monumenta Hispanica I: Correspondenz des Gobernadors von Castilien, Grossinquisitors
  von Spanien, Cardinals von Tortosa, Adrian von Utrecht mit Kaiser Karl V. im Jahre 1520", *Abhandlungen der
  königlich böhmischen Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften*, 6th series, 10 (Prague, 1881). The letter is
  no. XXIII, pp. 43-45, under the date 8 September (the letter is dated the 9th). After *por la cifra que va
  dentro desta* Höfler has "(Deest.)".
- Manuel Danvila y Collado, *Historia crítica y documentada de las Comunidades de Castilla*, vol. 2 (*Memorial
  Histórico Español* 36, Madrid, 1898), pp. 17-19. At the paragraph: "(Sigue un párrafo cifrado que no ha
  podido traducirse, y continúa:)". Vol. 1 (*Memorial Histórico Español* 35, Madrid, 1897) is cited here once,
  for fol. 156.

Three other texts used here are in vol. 4 of the same work (*Memorial Histórico Español* 38, Madrid, 1898):
the letter of 23 May 1521 from Segovia, whose third cipher paragraph, printed deciphered, is the paragraph of
letter D (pp. 28-29); the cipher paragraphs of the letter of 24 October 1521 (pp. 547-549; letter B); and the
letter from Logroño of 14 August 1521 (p. 379). Danvila cites the manuscripts of the first two as Comunidades
de Castilla, leg. 5, fols 398-401, and leg. 6, fol. 422. He prints the letters of 1 and 6 May 1521 (pp. 7-8
and 11-12) from the sheet that holds letter D, which he cites as Comunidades de Castilla, leg. 1, fol. 219,
and omits its cipher paragraph.

Danvila prints the cipher words of the letter of 8 October 1520 (vol. 2, pp. 251-252) in a decipherment that
he credits to Claudio Pérez y Gredilla, the head of the Simancas archive. They are in the cipher of the
paragraph, and Pérez y Gredilla's reading of them is the earliest modern one found of anything written in
this cipher. They are the second test of [METHOD.md](METHOD.md). The same archivist read the
governors' joint letter of 30 December 1521 (vol. 4, pp. 703-705), which is in a different cipher. Nothing of
his is printed for 9 September. Danvila's note calls him the author of an *Estudio de las claves* on which the
Real Academia de la Historia had reported favourably and which, he says, had not yet had from the government
the attention it deserved. If the study survives it may hold his working of this cipher.

Gustav Bergenroth's *Supplement to Volume I and Volume II of Letters, Despatches, and State Papers ... Spain*
(London, 1868) prints the cardinal's letters of 4, 14 and 23 September 1520 and passes over the one of the
9th.

The rising at Dueñas and the turn of the revolt against the lords are treated in Joseph Pérez, *La révolution
des « Comunidades » de Castille (1520-1521)* (Bordeaux, 1970), and Juan Ignacio Gutiérrez Nieto, *Las
Comunidades como movimiento antiseñorial* (Barcelona, 1973). Danvila gives the night of the rising only as "la
noche de un domingo" in September (vol. 2, p. 146); the new Comunidad of Dueñas wrote to Valladolid on 2
September (p. 148), and the Junta answered a complaint about the rising on the 4th (p. 45).

## The cipher

Olga Kolosova, *El lenguaje secreto de la diplomacia de Carlos V (1521-1527)*, doctoral thesis, Universitat de
València, supervised by Júlia Benavent. The title page is dated May 2016 and the thesis is usually cited as
2017. The full text is in Teseo, the Spanish ministry's database of theses. This cipher is "cifra 4" of the
thesis: the table of signs is on p. 227, the vocabulary on pp. 249-250, and the remarks on the first letters
of its groups, and on the order in which the secretaries compiled such vocabularies, on pp. 241, 243 and 248.
Page 229 describes the words in clear that open passages in cipher without being read. The cipher was rebuilt
from letters that Adrian wrote to Charles as pope between September 1522 and March 1523, now in the Real
Academia de la Historia.

A shorter version of the thesis, with the same title page, is in the University of Valencia's repository,
RODERIC (<http://hdl.handle.net/10550/66216>). It quotes only a few groups of this cipher. Lope Hurtado's
cipher is the example in its section on the assignment of codes, and its pages are not numbered.

Kolosova has since published a book on the same subject, *El lenguaje cifrado en tiempos de Carlos V
(1521-1527)* (Salamanca: Ediciones Universidad de Salamanca, 2024).

Satoshi Tomokiyo, "Scholarly Studies on Ciphers in the Reign of Emperor Charles V", *Cryptiana*,
<http://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/spanish2C.htm>, in the section dated 24 September 2026. The section
suggests that groups reported from the cardinal's Simancas letters of late 1521 belong to Kolosova's
vocabulary, which would carry the cipher back to 1521; adds that if they did not, the cipher would be a fourth
one, whose original should be inspected; and says that the letters could not be found on PARES. The table
above gives their numbers.

Dan Bourdeau, notes on the governors' joint letter of 30 December 1521, in the repository
<https://github.com/dbourdeau/cyphersolver> (`targets/adrian1521/NOTES.md`, and the write-up
`docs/adrian1521.html`, posted 18 September and revised 30 September 2026). The notes remark that
PTR,LEG,2,1,94, 99, 100 (with its wholly ciphered enclosure) and 101 are in a "dotted trigraph code" different
from that of the joint letter, and quote some groups of that code. Those are the groups Tomokiyo compared.

Mikel García Larragan, "Criptoanálisis a una carta cifrada del cardenal de Tortosa", four blog posts, 2-6
April 2026, beginning at
<https://mikelgarcialarragan.blogspot.com/2026/04/criptografia-cccxl-criptoanalisis-una.html>. The posts solve
the cardinal's letter of 28 July 1520, which is in a different cipher, whose cryptogram uses 53 distinct
signs. The cardinal was therefore using another cipher six weeks before the paragraph. When and why the cipher
studied here came into use has not been established. None of the works in this section mentions the letter of
9 September 1520.

## The search for an earlier reading

On 11 October 2026 a reading of the paragraph, or any of its phrases (*quitar todas las alcavalas*, *contra
todos los grandes*, *de poco tiempo aca fueron de la corona real*), was sought in the Internet Archive's scans
of Höfler and of all six volumes of Danvila (*Memorial Histórico Español* 35-40), through their machine-read
text; in Bergenroth's Supplement and Kolosova's thesis; in Tomokiyo's article above and the list of unsolved
historical ciphers on the same site; in Bourdeau's repository and its catalogue of solved and unsolved
letters; on García Larragan's blog; in the catalogue <https://github.com/aaymeloglu/unsolved-ciphers>, whose
file `catalogue/pares-hits.jsonl` lists PTR,LEG,2,1,19 among partly ciphered records on PARES and gives no
reading; and on the web at large and GitHub, by shelfmark, PARES number and phrase.

On 12 October 2026 the web, GitHub and Zenodo were searched again, and the pages of Tomokiyo, Bourdeau, García
Larragan and the catalogue above were read again as they then stood. In Bourdeau's repository the notes on the
cardinal's letters had not changed since 30 September except for `targets/adrian1521/profile.json`, on 11
October, and the later changes to the write-up touched only the site's menus. García Larragan's blog had no
further post on the cardinal's letters, and Tomokiyo's article had no later section on them.

Nothing was found.
