# The paragraph

Simancas, Archivo General, PTR,LEG,2,1,19, first page. The paragraph stands between the clear-text sentence
that ends *por la cifra que va dentro desta* and the one that begins *El Presidente era buelto a este lugar*.
Its first two words, *quanto mas*, are in clear, and the rest is cipher: six lines, the last a short one.

## Transcription

Groups are given in their own letters with the closing dot. Letter signs are given as a letter and a number,
for example `a2`, which is the second sign in the column for a in Kolosova's table. The two signs for q are
`q1` and `q2`. A slash is the clerk's word divider, and `[.]` in line 2 is a dot that belongs to no group.
[data/signs.tsv](../data/signs.tsv) describes the shape of each sign.

```
1  quanto mas q1 u3 i6 c1 a2 roc. d2 a3 r2 a4 fud. / a2 roc. s4 / m2 a1 l4 e3 s1
2  gig. leb. ded. mud. di. m1 u1 e1 u2 e4 n3 vuh. t1 r1 a2 / s3 u3 [.] mu. nah. yae.
3  vuh. t2 r3 a3 / cab. s4 / nof. qag. su. so. s1 / gof. raf. ru. n3 / p2 r3 i1 m2 e1 r1 a2
4  mud. q2 u5 i4 t2 a2 r2 / ceb. s4 / nad. a4 l4 c2 a3 u1 a2 l1 a1 s4 / i2 su. gad.
5  ceb. s3 / nad. u2 i3 ll1 a3 s1 i5 neg. raf. gig. sa. hag. cu. zi. qec. r1 o1 n3 so. a3 /
6  c1 o3 r3 o1 n3 a2 fed. /
```

In the groups the letters r and z, and q and g, are easily confused in this hand. [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) says
how they are distinguished.

The word *primeramente* begins at the end of line 3 and takes its ending, `mud.`, from the start of line 4.
Its m, `m2`, is a sign of Kolosova's table, but that the mark on the page is this sign is a judgement
(point 1 below). [METHOD.md](METHOD.md#the-cipher) says why `mu.` is read as *Magestad* on its own.

## The reading

> {Quanto mas} quiça esto dara remedio a estos males, que no solamente se mueven contra su Magestad mas ahun
> contra todos los grandes, dels quales entienden primeramente quitar todas las alcavalas y despues todas las
> villas y lugares que de poco tiempo aca fueron de la corona real.

Words spelt in letter signs are printed as the clerk spelt them. Where one sign serves for two letters the
choice is editorial: ç in *quiça*, v in *mueven*, *alcavalas* and *villas*, y for the conjunction. A group
carries no spelling, and its word is printed as the clear text of the letters spells it: *ahun* for `yae.` and
*Magestad* for `mu.`, where Kolosova has *aun* and *Majestad*. The braces mark the two words in clear with
which the paragraph opens ([METHOD.md](METHOD.md#words-in-clear-within-the-cipher)). In modern spelling, with
*dels* given as *de los*:

> {Cuanto más} quizá esto dará remedio a estos males, que no solamente se mueven contra Su Majestad mas aun
> contra todos los grandes, de los cuales entienden primeramente quitar todas las alcabalas y después todas las
> villas y lugares que de poco tiempo acá fueron de la corona real.

The paragraph calls the king *su Magestad*, "His Majesty". Elsewhere the letter addresses him as *vuestra
Magestad* or *vuestra Alteza* and does not use the title in the third person, though the passage after the
date has *su real stado* (Danvila, vol. 2, p. 18) and *a su real servicio* (p. 19). The signs give no other
reading.

The form *dels* is the group for *del* with an s. In the letter of 7 December 1521 the clerk makes *de los*
from the same group with an o and an s, so an o may be missing here. It is also the Catalan form,
and the clear text of the letter has *ciudat* and *verdat*, with the final t of Aragonese and of older
Castilian.

## Translation

> Perhaps this will bring a remedy to these ills, for they are rising not only against His Majesty but against
> all the grandees as well, from whom they mean to take first all the alcabalas and afterwards all the towns
> and villages that not long ago belonged to the royal Crown.

- If *quanto mas*, the two words in braces, belongs to the paragraph, it ties the paragraph to the hope that
  the cardinal expresses earlier in the letter.
- By *esto* the cardinal means the conduct of the rebels described in the lines before, who were withholding
  the royal revenues.
- The clause *que no solamente se mueven* could also be taken as relative, on *males*: "ills which are
  stirring not only against His Majesty". The sense is little altered.
- The turn *no solamente ... mas ahun* is one that the letter has just used in clear. A few lines above the
  paragraph it says of the rebels at Plasencia *no solamente de fecho detienen las pecunias y rentas reales,
  mas ahun han tomado* the money a tax-farmer's servant had collected.
- With an infinitive, *entienden* is "they intend". The cardinal uses it in the same way in the letter of
  5 December 1521, *entienden de juntar al dicho tiempo gruesso exercito*.
- Literally, *que de poco tiempo aca fueron de la corona real* is "which a short time since were the royal
  Crown's", that is, places that had been the Crown's until lately.

## Points that rest on judgement

Each of these places is enlarged in `images/details/`.

1. The m of *primeramente* (line 3, `m2`; [line3_primeramente.png](../images/details/line3_primeramente.png)).
   The sign has the diagonal and the loop of the usual m but lacks its top bar, and without the bar it is
   close to the form of t that is missing from the table (in *contentaria* in the first test passage). Read as
   t it would give *priteramente*, which is not a word. In letter A the m without its bar begins *mil* twice
   ([image 2, line 26](../images/parallels/A2_26-30.jpg);
   [image 4, line 1](../images/parallels/A4_01-04.jpg)), and the decipherment of 1521 has *mil* at both
   places. Elsewhere in that letter *mil* is written with the usual forms of m.
2. The dot in line 2 ([line2_su_dot_mu.png](../images/details/line2_su_dot_mu.png)). After the two signs
   `s3 u3` there is a dot, and the group `mu.` follows. Letter signs take no dot, so it does not belong to
   them. Nor are the two signs a group, since the first of them, `s3`, is a letter sign for s, shaped like a q
   with two bars across the stem.
3. The first sign of *quiça* (line 1, `q1`; [line1_quica_esto.png](../images/details/line1_quica_esto.png)).
   It is made of the sign for p and a tall long s with a ring, joined by one bar, and of the signs in the
   table the second part comes closest to one of those for o. Read as p and o the word would be *pouica*.
   [METHOD.md](METHOD.md#the-letter-q) gives the four places in the letters of 1521 where the pair stands
   for q.
4. The group `roc.` at its first occurrence (line 1, in the same enlargement). The middle letter is slightly
   blotted. It is read as o, like the unblotted `roc.` later in the same line. With an a the group would be
   `rac.` (*embajador*).
5. The g of `gof.` (line 3; [line3_qag_gof.png](../images/details/line3_qag_gof.png)). The group has the loop
   of `gig.`, and `qag.`, three groups earlier in the same line, has the straight q. With a q it would be
   `qof.`, *gouernadores* ([METHOD.md](METHOD.md#letters-that-are-easily-confused)). The second reader of the
   paragraph also read g here.
